![](images/agile-pills.png)

A lot of people in software development are still struggling what Agile is all about and what it is?

Many of you heard of the Agile principles like: sprints, time-boxing, scrum and Kanban but are not sure on how these connect to each other and what Agile is all about.

Most People are interested and triggered by some of the claims that are made by working 'Agile'.  Claims like: [31% faster completion then industry average](http://www.infoq.com/news/2012/11/success-agile-projects)

Basically, Agile  is all about innovation.  However, the Agile method is not the silver bullit for every situation.  It's not optimally  suited for routine operations and processes. Agile really shines in areas where:

- Market environments that change frequently
- Close collaboration  with customers is possible and requirements are not clear
- Problems are complex, solutions are unknown, and the scope isn't clearly defined
- Work can be broken into parts and conducted in rapid, iterative cycles.
- Late changes are manageable

and mistakes that are not directly catastrophic

The Agile methodology can be broken down in three principles (in no particular order):

### __scrum__

emphasizes on creative and adaptive teamwork in solving complex problems

### _lean development_

focuses on the continual elimination of waste

### _kanban_

concentrates on reducing lead times and the amount of work in process

But for Agile teams to be successful they requires training, behavioural change, and often new information technologies. Managers  must decide whether the anticipated play-offs will justify the effort and expense of such a transition.
